South Sudan · 79 counties · 2018–2025

Climate Trends in South Sudan

How water, heat, land and air have changed across South Sudan since 2018, shown next to conflict, displacement, hunger and aid funding. Built from NASA and other satellite records and from UN and research datasets.

Executive summary

This dashboard brings together 18 climate and environment measures and 10 measures of conflict, food and aid for each of South Sudan’s 79 counties, month by month from January 2018 to December 2025. For each county and measure, the map shows the trend: the typical change per year since 2018, estimated with Sen’s slope and tested for significance using the Mann-Kendall test. For monthly data, each month is compared with the same month of 2018, controlling for seasonality. Where a test shows the trend is unlikely to be chance, the county is outlined.

The clearest signal is water. Since 2018, the land has been holding more water, flood water has spread further across the Sudd wetland, and floods and other disasters have displaced hundreds of thousands of people in most years. Over the same period, money for the UN aid appeal fell sharply in 2025, when US government funding dropped to less than half of its 2024 level.

Reading the trends with care. Eight years is a short record. A trend describes 2018–2025 and should not be extended into the future. Some sources start later than 2018 or have gaps (for example, MODIS has no data for July–October 2025, and IOM Event Tracking starts in 2020). Counts of events depend on what gets reported. Each measure’s caveats are shown when you select it on the map.
